Key Projects

Research Apr 2024 - Present

Hierarchical Probabilistic Modeling for LC-MS/MS Proteomics

Developing a novel approach to peptide and protein identification and quantification using posterior inference in a hierarchical mixture-of-mixtures model grounded in the physical processes underlying LC-MS data generation, through the model formulation, objective function, and prior distributions. Implemented the model in PyTorch, exploiting data sparsity and GPU acceleration, and validated it on experimental LC-MS data. The unified generative framework identified and quantified additional peptides while agreeing with established pipelines on shared identifications and quantitative trends.

PyTorch Probabilistic modeling Proteomics EM algorithm Optimal transport
Internship Chugai Pharmaceutical

Multimodal Learning for Protein-Ligand Binding Affinity Prediction

Evaluated the multimodal foundation model MAMMAL with a protein-family-level out-of-distribution split. Despite strong overall performance, it showed a negative correlation between predicted and measured affinities for unseen protein families. Proposed a model integrating pretrained ESM-2 and ChemBERTa via cross-attention, which matched MAMMAL's overall performance with about one-quarter of the parameters and generalized more robustly to unseen protein families.

Internship PFN

Causal Gene-Network Inference with LLMs

Investigated whether biological knowledge encoded in LLMs can support gene-network reconstruction, as an alternative to costly expert curation behind databases such as KEGG. On a 19-gene breast-cancer KEGG pathway, a pairwise GPT-5 Nano workflow outperformed conventional causal-discovery methods.

In Silico Design of Cardiomyocyte Differentiation-Inducing Compounds from hiPSCs

Research Assistant in the Packwood Group, iCeMS. Used machine learning to predict compounds that induce cardiomyocyte differentiation from human iPS cells, and designed candidate molecules by decomposing known inducers into medicinal-chemistry-informed fragments and recombining them. Experimental follow-up confirmed that a designed compound induced cardiomyocyte differentiation, leading to a peer-reviewed publication.
