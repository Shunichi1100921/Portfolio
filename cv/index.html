Research profile

Master’s student at the Graduate School of Pharmaceutical Sciences, Kyoto University, developing computational models for proteomics and drug discovery. Began research in a predominantly experimental laboratory with limited prior knowledge of statistics and proteomics, building the necessary expertise through self-directed study. Integrated this knowledge into physically grounded generative model formulations, designed an evaluation framework, and implemented scalable models with GPU acceleration. Validated these models on real experimental data, establishing a new analytical approach to modeling the generation of LC–MS data from biological samples. Also applied statistical and machine learning methods to protein–ligand binding affinity prediction. Drew on pharmaceutical and statistical knowledge to evaluate model performance and analyze model behavior, and combined domain insights with machine learning methods to propose and implement new models. Proposed evaluation criteria and models better aligned with practical drug discovery needs. Particularly interested in learning latent molecular properties from experimental measurements, sequences, and structures to advance drug discovery.

Research Experience

Kyoto University

Hierarchical Probabilistic Modeling for LC-MS/MS Proteomics

Apr 2024 - Present

  • Developed a novel approach to peptide and protein identification and quantification using posterior inference in a hierarchical mixture-of-mixtures model grounded in the physical processes underlying LC-MS data generation, through the model formulation, objective function, and prior distributions. Implemented the model in PyTorch, exploiting data sparsity and GPU acceleration for efficient computation, and validated it on experimental LC-MS data.
  • Demonstrated that this unified generative framework could identify and quantify additional peptides while maintaining agreement with established pipelines for shared identifications and quantitative trends, providing a proof of concept for modeling LC-MS data directly from its underlying physical processes.

Chugai Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd.

Research Intern · Multimodal Learning for Protein-Ligand Binding Affinity Prediction

Aug 2025 - Oct 2025

  • Evaluated multimodal foundation model MAMMAL on protein-ligand binding affinity prediction using a protein-family-level out-of-distribution evaluation motivated by the imbalance in protein family representation in the dataset, which revealed that, despite strong overall performance, MAMMAL exhibited a negative correlation between predicted and measured affinities for unseen protein families, highlighting a substantial limitation in its ability to generalize to new targets.
  • Proposed a model that integrates pretrained ESM-2 and ChemBERTa through cross-attention to capture local interactions between amino acid residues and ligand atoms. With approximately one-quarter of the parameters of MAMMAL, the model achieved comparable overall predictive performance while demonstrating more robust generalization to unseen protein families.

Kyoto University iCeMS · Packwood Group

Research Assistant · In Silico Candidate Molecule Design

Jun 2022 - Mar 2023

  • Used machine learning to predict compounds that induce cardiomyocyte differentiation from human induced pluripotent stem (iPS) cells, addressing the need for molecular environments that reliably direct differentiation toward a desired cell type.

Preferred Networks, Inc.

Research Intern · Causal Gene-Network Inference with LLMs

Aug 2025 - Sep 2025

  • Investigated whether biological knowledge encoded in LLMs could support gene-network reconstruction, motivated by the importance of these networks for understanding disease mechanisms and identifying drug targets, and by the high cost of expert manual curation underlying major databases such as KEGG.
